Situation Report

Summary of the Morticaius outbreak and the B.L.A.C.K.O.U.T. Unit's primary directive.

During the height of the 1970s Cold War, an expedition discovers the remains of an ancient civilization beneath the ocean surrounding the corpse of an unidentified lifeform.

The specimen is recovered and transported to a secure government laboratory. Researchers discover that its body is being consumed, mutated, and biologically rebuilt by an unknown disease temporarily designated Morticaius.

Microscopic Morticaius colonies spread through ventilation systems, drainage pipes, laboratory equipment, contaminated surfaces, animals, and isolated personnel. Emergency lockdowns fail until only Lab 19 remains operational.

The United States establishes the B.L.A.C.K.O.U.T. Unit: a small, underfunded team of elite operatives ordered to eliminate infected threats, rescue survivors, recover research, and develop a cure.

You are the unit's newest operative. Alongside story-driven AI squadmates, you are deployed to Lab 19 after its captured human specimen breaches containment and all communication with the facility collapses.

FILE 03 / 07

Incident History

A discovery beneath the ocean becomes humanity's greatest biological containment crisis.

During the height of the 1970s Cold War, an expedition discovers the remains of an ancient underwater civilization surrounding the corpse of an unidentified lifeform. The specimen is recovered and transported to a secure government laboratory, where scientists realize it is being consumed and mutated by an unknown biological disease they temporarily designate Morticaius.

Every Morticaius cell functions as skin, blood, and nerve tissue simultaneously. A single cell can survive independently, enter a living subject, consume it from within, and rebuild the organism until nothing of the original host remains.

Researchers initially believe the mature creature depends on a normal heart. They later discover that nearly its entire interior is a living circulatory organ. This internal heart mass contracts like a sponge to move billions of living cells while a thick outer layer of muscle protects it.

Because the head contains less circulatory heart tissue, damaging the head is generally less effective than causing catastrophic damage to the creature's internal body.

After twelve captive specimens are destroyed, three surviving cells infect a sheep and regenerate a complete organism within one day. The discovery proves that apparent death does not guarantee containment.

Microscopic colonies remain hidden inside ventilation shafts, drainage pipes, laboratory equipment, and contaminated surfaces. They consume animals, biological waste, and isolated personnel, silently rebuilding until multiple laboratories are overrun.

Only Lab 19 continues transmitting. Its researchers report the capture of a severely mutated human moments before the specimen breaches containment. Communication collapses, and the newly formed B.L.A.C.K.O.U.T. Unit is sent into its first operation.

Confirmed Biology

  • Cells perform skin, blood, and neural functions
  • One surviving cell may remain independently alive
  • Living hosts are consumed and biologically replaced
  • Most of the interior acts as a circulatory heart mass
  • Protective muscle surrounds the internal heart tissue
  • The head contains less vulnerable circulatory tissue
  • Heavy internal-body damage is generally most effective
  • Three cells survived the destruction of twelve specimens
  • Full host conversion may occur within twenty-four hours

Recovered Research Logs

Selected transcripts recovered from the Morticaius research program and the final transmissions from Lab 19.

LOG 01 Origin Archive
Recovered
We found it in the depths of the ocean. It was some unknown being surrounded by what was almost an entire city built underwater. Once we brought the body back to our laboratories, we discovered that it was being consumed and mutated by some kind of disease.
LOG 07 Cellular Study
Restricted
Their bodies are made of cells that function not only as skin, but as neurons and blood cells. One cell on its own could be its own living being. They are a disease. I will call them Morticaius for now. Morticaius replicate inside a subject, eating it and becoming it afterward. Mature organisms become predators and consume anything they can reach. At this stage they develop muscle and what appears to be a heart to circulate the cells. We believe destroying this heart kills the organism because the cells cannot return to their independent state quickly enough.
LOG 08 Survival Trial
Critical
What I said before about Morticaius dying is not always the case. Out of twelve captive specimens we destroyed, three cells survived. We placed a sheep inside the chamber with those cells. It took only one day for the animal to become fully Morticaius. Apparent death does not guarantee containment.

Protocol amended: all remains must be treated as living contamination.

LOG 09 Lockdown Record
Emergency
I did everything right, yet it still got out. All laboratories have been under lockdown for weeks, but the specimens still broke free. Only Lab 19 remains intact. The higher-ups are discussing the creation of a unit to correct my mistake. They call it the B.L.A.C.K.O.U.T. Unit—a small team of the most skilled operatives the United States can assemble.
LOG 12 Final Transmission
Lab 19
B.L.A.C.K.O.U.T. has been founded. Their directive is to neutralize the threats, save the living, and develop a cure. They have little funding now, but that should change if they can prove the unit is worth supporting. Most importantly, we captured one. It is human—or it was. Its arms are so swollen that it almost looks as if it has no body at all. What happened to them is terrible. We plan to terminate it here in the laboratory and study what remains.

Transmission terminated shortly before the captured subject breached containment.
